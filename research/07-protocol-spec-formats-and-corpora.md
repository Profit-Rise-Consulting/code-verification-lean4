### A2. IETF formal-methods efforts

- **Usable Formal Methods Research Group (UFMRG, IRTF).** Charter objectives include exploring "strengths and limitations of formal methods ... for specification and implementation of algorithms, protocols, and systems specified in the IETF" and "tools and techniques to help derive formal models from natural language and semi-structured specifications". Targets listed: algorithms, protocols at all layers, security protocols, cryptographic algorithms, APIs, distributed systems. [P] https://datatracker.ietf.org/rg/ufmrg/about/ ; charter doc: https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/charter-irtf-ufmrg/ [P]
- First met at IETF 116 (29 Mar 2023) per IETF blog: "aims to bring together the Internet protocol standards development community and the academic research community studying formal methods of protocol specification". [P] https://www.ietf.org/blog/ietf116-new-topics/
- UFMRG sample-problem draft: draft-farrell-ufmrg-sample-00 proposes IMAP search as an IETF-relevant sample problem. [P] https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-farrell-ufmrg-sample-00
- IETF 123 UFMRG slides on EverParse ("eꓦerpꓥrse"): Project Everest ~1 million lines of F*; "Every network packet passing through Hyper-V is validated by EverParse formally verified"; mentions 3D, CDDL frontends. [P] https://datatracker.ietf.org/meeting/123/materials/slides-123-ufmrg-eerprse-00
- IETF 116 irtfopen minutes: formal-methods group listed as assisting "specification, design, analysis, and implementation of network protocols". [P] https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/minutes-116-irtfopen/
- IRTFopen talks on SCALE (IETF 117 slides; IETF 118 minutes). Minutes quote the author: "The model was derived manually from the RFCs, in 2020. It's a hard labour." [P] https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/minutes-118-irtfopen/04/ ; slides: https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/slides-117-irtfopen-scale-automatically-finding-rfc-compliance-bugs-in-dns-nameservers/ [P]
- Survey-style claim on RFC 9000: "we have found in practice that a small number of formal methods experts can effectively capture the formalizations of complex protocols, such as QUIC, and keep up with changes as a protocol evolves." (Section 3.3 of arXiv 2509.13208). [P, but a position/survey paper] https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13208v1
- RFC 9000 (QUIC) itself: Proposed Standard, May 2021, 151 pages. [P] https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/rfc9000/ ; WG documents: https://datatracker.ietf.org/group/quic/documents/
- "FMBF" (as named in the task brief): **not found** in any source read; see Open Questions.

### A3. Lean-adjacent artifacts that consume protocol formats (evidence only)

- **LeanMqtt** - "work-in-progress formally verified parser and serializer for the MQTT 5.0 network protocol, written in Lean 4"; dependent-type packet model; proofs of parser roundtrip (completeness) and reconstruction (soundness). [P] https://github.com/edusporto/lean-mqtt
- SBLP 2026 paper (published 2026-09-08, pp. 179-184), Porto and de Melo: "Foundations for Formally Verified Network Protocol Parsers in Lean 4: A Case Study with MQTT". Stated verified properties: completeness (roundtrip) and soundness (reconstruction) w.r.t. a serializer. Stated aim: "generalized, embedded domain-specific language for generating verified network protocol parsers". Page does not list exclusions. [P] https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/sblp/article/view/46205
- **Veil** - "foundational framework for specifying, implementing, testing, and proving safety (and, in the future, liveness) properties of state transition systems, with a focus on distributed protocols", embedded in Lean 4; SMT discharge via cvc5/Z3 for decidable fragments plus interactive Lean proofs. [P] https://veil.dev/docs/ ; CAV paper: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-031-98682-6_2
  - Reported: 16 case studies; "all but 2 benchmarks in under 15 s (87.5%)"; verifies Single-Decree Paxos, Vertical Paxos, Suzuki-Kasami, Reliable Broadcast "outside EPR"; Ivy "succeeds in verifying Reliable Broadcast and Suzuki-Kasami and times out for Paxos and Vertical Paxos" (CAV paper, P). Slides by L. de Moura: "All 16 verified. Ivy failed on 2." [P] https://leodemoura.github.io/static/nfm2026/
- **Aeneas** (Rust -> pure functional model) has backends for F*, Coq, HOL4 and Lean; maintainers' project page "particularly recommend the use of the Lean backend". [P] https://github.com/AeneasVerif/aeneas ; https://aeneasverif.github.io/projects/ . Lean-backend crypto tech report (Sept 2026 draft): backend of "29k lines of Lean". [P] https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15648v1 . Rust->Lean pipeline with AI provers (Charon/Aeneas or Hax -> Lean 4): [P] https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30106 . Aeneas case study on jxl-rs: [S/P blog by author] https://jonathan.protzenko.fr/2026/05/05/jxl-rs.html
- **Lean Ethereum** pattern page: "Formal verification tooling (Lean 4) used to prove key properties of the signature aggregation and consensus logic." [S] https://ethsystems.org/patterns/pattern-lean-ethereum/
- **pb64-lean** GitHub org: "Lean 4 libraries and build tools for networked services: HTTP, gRPC, WebSockets" (excerpt only); pg-lean README: SCRAM-SHA-256(-PLUS) "pure-Lean crypto, RFC 7677-vector-tested". These are described as libraries/tests; no proof claim seen in the excerpt. [P, excerpt] https://github.com/pb64-lean ; https://github.com/pb64-lean/pg-lean/blob/main/README.md
- Lean docs (Theorem Proving in Lean 4, intro): claims "network protocols" among things Lean can verify. [P] https://lean-lang.org/theorem_proving_in_lean4///Introduction/
